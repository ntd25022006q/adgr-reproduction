\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Problem}
Large language models answer from memory, which causes hallucinations on
specialized topics such as medicine. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
fixes this by fetching evidence documents before answering. Standard RAG
fetches isolated passages by keyword or embedding similarity, which misses
evidence connected through relationships (a drug \textit{contraindicated for}
a condition, a symptom \textit{caused by} a disease). Graph-based RAG
organizes evidence as a knowledge graph of entities and relations, but naive
graph expansion drifts into irrelevant neighborhoods and drowns the answer in
noise.
\subsection{What the paper proposes}
Adaptive Directed Graph Retrieval (ADGR): retrieval guided at every step by
query relevance --- hybrid anchors for reliable entry points, semantic edge
filtering to stop drift, multi-source aggregation for coverage, and a
cross-encoder rerank for final ordering. Plus ViMedGraph, a public Vietnamese
medical graph resource.
\subsection{What this reproduction does}
We rebuilt the entire system from scratch on one NVIDIA A30: corpus,
knowledge graph, all five retrieval variants, two baselines, and all eight
evaluation tables on the same 300 questions. Every substitution is disclosed;
every gap is measured, not hidden.

%====================================================================
\section{Paper content: the ADGR method}
\subsection{Pipeline overview}
For a question $q$: (1) an LLM extracts low-level keywords $K_l$ (exact
disease/drug/symptom phrases) and high-level keywords $K_h$ (query intent);
(2) hybrid anchor retrieval finds reliable graph entry entities by combining
BM25 lexical match with dense embedding match plus an overlap bonus
($\delta=0.1$) for entities found by both channels; (3) one-hop edges around
anchors are scored jointly ($\alpha=0.3$ anchor confidence, $\beta=0.7$
query-edge similarity) and filtered ($\tau_{edge}=0.3$, quota $m=10$ per
anchor, global cap $N_{max}=50$), while global relations ($n_g=15$) and direct
dense chunks ($n_s=10$) are fetched in parallel; (4) the four chunk sets
($T_v,T_e,T_g,T_s$) merge with round-robin dedup, reranked by Qwen3-Reranker,
top $n_c=10$ within 30,000 tokens; (5) Qwen2.5-14B generates at temperature 0.
\subsection{Main contributions of the paper}
C1: query-aware control at anchor selection \textit{and} graph expansion
(the two stages where errors propagate most). C2: the public ViMedGraph
resource (1,932 documents / 17,236 chunks / 49,274 entities / 106,666
relations). C3: full evaluation (retrieval + RAGAS + 5-run judge + efficiency
+ ablations + case study).
\subsection{Experimental design of the paper}
300 ViMedAQA questions; ADGR vs LightRAG Hybrid/Mix (their native output,
no rerank); 7 standalone LLMs as non-retrieval references; manual gold-chunk
labels; official RAGAS (faithfulness, answer relevancy, context recall,
context precision); GPT-5.5 judge on 4 dimensions $\times$ 5 runs at temp 0;
tokens and latency.

%====================================================================
\section{Our reproduction workflow}
Crawl 1,934 YouMed articles $\rightarrow$ clean HTML $\rightarrow$ H2-section
chunking (15,837 chunks) $\rightarrow$ extract entities/relations with
Qwen2.5-14B temp 0 in \texttt{<|\#|>} format under the paper's 10+1 schema
$\rightarrow$ build matrices/BM25/chunk maps $\rightarrow$ run 300 questions
$\times$ 5 pipeline variants $\rightarrow$ LightRAG baselines from saved
contexts $\rightarrow$ judge every answer (minimal-effort escalation, quota
guard) $\rightarrow$ official-algorithm RAGAS $\rightarrow$ tokens/latency
$\rightarrow$ merge 8 tables + 10 figures. A 16-stage chain runs everything
with per-question checkpoints (any crash loses $\le$1 row), resume, and
post-stage validation gates that halt loudly on malformed output instead of
passing garbage downstream. Every code change was verified on the remote
machine before acceptance (41 fixes, all evidenced).

%====================================================================
\section{Main contributions of this reproduction}
R1: rebuilt knowledge graph at 92--107\% of paper scale \textit{with} all four
quality metrics recomputed (DSER 0.913, SCR 0.884, LCR 0.816). R2: corrected
two ablation arms that violated Sec.~5.8 definitions. R3: reimplemented the
official RAGAS algorithms. R4: fetched the original judge prompt (Online
Resource 1) and quantified the prompt effect ($-0.09$ scale shift, rankings
kept). R5: decomposed the faithfulness gap by experiment (two-step 0.77 vs
one-step 0.41 vs paper 0.89). R6: operating rules distilled from real failures.

%====================================================================
\section{Function of each component}
\textbf{Crawler:} 1,934 article URLs $\rightarrow$ title, H2 hierarchy, clean
text, source URL. \textbf{Chunker:} one H2 section = one chunk (matches the
authors' chunker at 91.9\%). \textbf{Extractor:} per chunk, entities (name,
type, description) + relations (source, target, keywords, description) + source
chunk id. \textbf{Index:} normalized embedding matrices, BM25 over entity
names+descriptions, entity/relation$\rightarrow$chunk maps. \textbf{Keyword
LLM:} low/high keyword split with overlap-aware anchors. \textbf{Edge
filter:} joint anchor-edge scoring with threshold/quota/cap. \textbf{Merge:}
round-robin interleave + dedup. \textbf{Reranker:} cross-encoder top-10.
\textbf{Generator:} Qwen2.5-14B temp 0. \textbf{Judges:} Hit/MRR/nDCG (slug +
single gold proxies), RAGAS-4, 4-dimension $\times$ 5-run judge, tokens and
latency timers.

\section{Strengths}
Of the paper: relevance control at both error-propagation stages; compact
contexts ($-67\%$ tokens) that also cut generation latency; honest ablations;
public resources. Of this reproduction: Table-2 config lock; gates blocking
corrupt outputs; dual-proxy cross-checks; every substitution disclosed;
faithfulness gap decomposed by experiment rather than speculated.

%====================================================================
\section{Weaknesses and limitations}
Of the paper (Sec.~6.3, their own words): KG-quality dependence; fixed top-K
budget that may over- or under-supply evidence. Of this reproduction: manual
gold labels replaced by proxies (mutual agreement measured); solar-pro4 judge
instead of GPT-5.5 (calibration shift $-0.09$, rankings kept); different
standalone models (references only); lower faithfulness (short answers match
at 0.89; the gap concentrates in long answers under a stricter judge).

%====================================================================
\section{Proposed improvements}
(1) \textit{Adaptive pipeline:} skip graph traversal for easy questions ---
dense-only already nears full scores on single-gold ($\sim$10$\times$
cheaper). (2) \textit{Rank-fusion reranking:} rerank as a second opinion, not
an override (rerank drags single-gold Hit@1 $0.57\rightarrow0.25$).
(3) \textit{Sufficiency stopping:} stop expanding when evidence suffices ---
the paper's own stated future work. (4) \textit{Length-controlled
generation:} short answers already match paper faithfulness. Each variant
$\sim$4h on the ready infrastructure, with its own ablation column.

%====================================================================
\section{Significance}
Scientifically: an independent, verified confirmation of ADGR's mechanisms,
rankings, and ablation directions --- plus a reusable verification template
(gates, dual proxies, gap decomposition) for any RAG paper. Practically: a
52k-entity Vietnamese medical KG, a runnable chain, and operating rules
distilled from 41 real failures --- permanent assets, each new idea costing
only marginal compute.

\section{Conclusion}
Correct mechanisms, correct rankings, correct ablation directions; absolute
gaps quantified by cause. No mocked data, no cherry-picked rows, no invented
numbers --- including the numbers less flattering than the paper's, each with
a measured explanation.
